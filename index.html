Written for a sailboat. Shared because it worked.

Not a Silicon Valley pitch deck. A dockside necessity that escaped the marina.

After a career writing software, the hardest “systems” problem was not in a server room — it was a boat. Insurance photos after a storm. Engine hours you swear you logged. A friend’s boat that “just needs a look.” The quiet panic of a weather window.

So he built what he needed: a captain’s log that remembers weather, a map that understands radar, maintenance trails that a yard can trust, and handoffs that don’t die in a text thread. Then other people asked for the same tools — for their boats, their yards, their crews, and eventually for land trades that live by the same rules: show up, document, communicate, get paid.

Intrepid Marine LLC is that story turned into a product family. Small company. Serious craft. Built by someone who still has varnish under his nails (metaphorically — unless it’s varnishing season).

“If the app can’t survive a windy slip, a dead battery, and a tech who hates typing — it isn’t ready for boat people.”

What we believe

Boat ownership is a full-contact sport of logistics, weather, money, and memory. Software should reduce friction, not invent new ceremonies.

On-device first Your data lives with you — useful offline at the marina.
Field-tested Workflows shaped by real jobs, not mockups of imaginary ones.
Handoff ready PDF for humans, packages for techs who already have the app.
Family of apps Mariner → yard → land trades share a philosophy of proof.

The unglamorous challenges of boat ownership

Marketing fluff? Maybe. But every line is a bruise we already earned.

Weather that doesn’t wait

Windows open and close. You need station weather, seas, tides, and radar without a science degree.

Memory that lies

Was that oil change at 420 hours or 440? Who was aboard? What did the surveyor say last spring?

Yards speak work orders

A friendly text isn’t a handoff. Photos, problem statements, and a package the tech can import matter.

Friend’s boat syndrome

Helping a neighbor turns into unpaid project management — unless the documentation is clean.

Parts, delays, proof

Waiting on a gasket is fine. Waiting without a paper trail is how weekends evaporate.

One phone, many trades

The same discipline that runs a boat also runs an electrician’s day and a night gig at a venue.
